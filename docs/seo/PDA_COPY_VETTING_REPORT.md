# PDA copy vetting report

Status: Complete — copy reviewed, not implemented  
Reviewed: 2026-08-29

## Files reviewed

- `docs/seo/PDA_DEFINITION_PAGE_COPY_DRAFT.md`
- `docs/seo/WAITER_PDA_PAGE_COPY_DRAFT.md`

## Verdict

The drafts read like direct Greek website copy written for a shop owner, not like a generic software-company article. They are ready for Robert's voice review before implementation.

No detector can prove whether text was written by a person or a model. This review therefore does not try to “beat” a detector. It checks the things that actually make copy sound artificial: vague claims, corporate language, repetitive rhythm, padded introductions, perfect-looking lists, forced enthusiasm and claims that are not grounded in the product.

## What was checked

### 1. Natural Greek

- Short sentences are mixed with longer explanatory ones.
- The copy speaks directly to the owner using ordinary words such as `κινητό`, `υπολογιστής`, `βάρδια`, `κατάστημα` and `αν σου κάνει`.
- The tone is calm and practical. It does not sound translated from an English SaaS page.
- Greek spelling and monotonic accents are consistent.

### 2. AI and corporate phrasing

The drafts contain none of the usual filler terms or constructions such as:

- `ολοκληρωμένη λύση`;
- `καινοτόμο`;
- `απρόσκοπτη εμπειρία`;
- `βελτιστοποιεί`;
- `στη σημερινή εποχή`;
- `με λίγα λόγια`;
- `εν κατακλείδι`;
- `κορυφαίο` or `πρωτοποριακό`;
- fake urgency, excessive enthusiasm or exclamation marks.

### 3. Specificity

Every important statement is tied to something concrete:

- Windows x64 computer;
- phones or tablets on the local network;
- 30-day trial without card or personal information;
- 23,90 € per month including VAT after the trial;
- QR menu in the same subscription;
- compatible thermal printers installed in Windows;
- POSPal is not a fiscal POS or cash register.

The copy does not promise universal printer compatibility, a connection that works on every Wi-Fi setup, or an integration with the fiscal system.

### 4. Reader intent

The definition page answers `PDA τι είναι` immediately. It does not pretend that every reader is ready to buy. It gives an owner a clear path toward the commercial pages without turning the definition into a sales pitch.

The waiter-PDA page starts with the buying decision: whether the owner can use ordinary phones or tablets instead of buying a dedicated device. Price, requirements, trial and limitations appear before the visitor has to hunt for them.

### 5. Sales pressure

There are no invented customer stories, fake quotations, unsupported savings claims or claims that POSPal is better than every alternative. The final ask is proportionate: download it, connect one device and try a real order.

### 6. Repetition and rhythm

Repeated product facts are kept only where a visitor may need them to make a decision, especially trial terms, price and the non-fiscal boundary. Headings do not all use the same formula. Lists are used for practical scanning, not to make the copy look artificially comprehensive.

### 7. Conversion clarity

- The primary action on the commercial page is `Κατέβασε για Windows`.
- The definition page remains a feeder and does not force the download too early.
- Setup requirements are visible before the final CTA.
- The copy explains what happens after the 30-day trial.
- The visitor is not sent toward a demo funnel.

## Edits made during vetting

- Replaced broad wording such as `ολόκληρη τη λύση` with the more natural `όλο το σύστημα`.
- Used `αν σου κάνει` instead of polished marketing language.
- Rewrote the network explanation in the way an owner would describe it: the phone must be able to “see” the Windows computer.
- Simplified the temporary-connection explanation and stated its limitation plainly.
- Made printer compatibility cautious and practical: perform a real test print before using it during a shift.
- Added the self-service point without turning it into a slogan: the owner can download the software without speaking to a salesperson first.
- Removed exclamation marks and avoided forced punchlines. The ad can carry more attitude; the search pages need to earn trust.

## Remaining human decision

Robert should read the two drafts once for founder voice. The useful question is not “does this sound impressive?” It is: “Would I actually say this to a café owner standing in front of me?” Any sentence that fails that test should be marked before implementation.
