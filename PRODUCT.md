# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Greek hospitality owners, managers, and operators who already use or are actively looking for order-taking software. They are often evaluating the site between shifts or while preparing a new or seasonal venue. They want a direct, transparent, locally installed Windows solution and need to know quickly whether it fits their equipment, workflow, and ability to self-install.

The primary job is to choose, download, install, and start using POSPal without a distributor or sales call. Staff members and setup helpers are secondary users of the onboarding material.

## Product Purpose

POSPal provides table ordering, simple ordering without tables, kitchen workflow, and a QR menu in one direct monthly subscription. It runs alongside the venue's existing fiscal system and does not replace a fiscal POS or cash register.

The website exists to turn relevant Greek hospitality traffic into successful Windows downloads and guided first use. Success is not a button click alone: the visitor should understand the requirements, download the correct installer, follow the onboarding path, and reach a confident first live shift.

## Brand Personality

Direct, capable, grounded.

The site should feel like a practical tool built by people who understand a real hospitality shift. It should create confidence and control without looking corporate, cheap, overexcited, or dependent on sales theatre.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages with gradients, floating cards, decorative dashboards, and vague transformation claims.
- Enterprise POS sites with crowded mega-navigation, demo-request funnels, compliance theatre, and hidden pricing.
- Hospitality templates built around stock photos of smiling staff instead of real workflow evidence.
- Repetitive icon-card grids, excessive rounded containers, glass effects, fake metrics, and invented testimonials.
- Editorial or luxury aesthetics that make setup information harder to scan.
- Demo-first navigation that competes with the Windows download and setup guides.

## Design Principles

1. **Show operational truth.** Use real POSPal screens, equipment, order flow, and concrete Greek hospitality scenarios instead of abstract claims.
2. **Earn the download.** Explain fit, requirements, price, product boundaries, and what happens next before asking for commitment.
3. **Treat onboarding as product proof.** The installation path should be visible, complete, and reassuring throughout the marketing journey.
4. **One dominant action.** Download for Windows is primary; guides are the supporting action; proof never becomes a competing funnel.
5. **Prefer clarity with character.** Strong visual hierarchy and a specific operational point of view should replace both bland minimalism and decorative excess.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Maintain readable monotonic Greek, strong text contrast, visible keyboard focus, semantic headings and controls, 320px reflow, usable zoom, touch targets of at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, descriptive alternatives for product imagery, and reduced-motion behavior for every animation. Core download and guide actions must remain usable when JavaScript or motion is unavailable.
